Noun

8 types

Naming words.

Types

01
Countable Noun
Can be counted, one by one.
02
Uncountable Noun
Cannot be counted individually.
Eg: hairs, stars
03
Proper Noun
The specific name of a person, place, or thing.
04
Common Noun
A general name for a person, place, or thing.
05
Abstract Noun
A feeling or emotion — cannot be touched.
Eg: happy
06
Concrete Noun
Something that can be seen or touched.
Eg: gold, table
07
Material Noun
Made up of a substance or material.
08
Collective Noun
Refers to a whole group as one unit.

Pronoun

Words which we use instead of a noun.

Types

01
Personal Pronoun
Eg: he, she, they, we
02
Demonstrative Pronoun
Eg: that, this, those, these, it
03
Relative Pronoun
All the "wh‑" words.
Eg: who, whom, where, which
04
Possessive Pronoun
Eg: Ali's pen · This book is mine

Eg: myself, himself

Verb

6 types

Action, or state of being. Without a verb we cannot make a sentence.

Types

01
Modal Verb
Expresses possibility, permission, or order.
02
Regular Verb
Forms the past tense with "‑ed" / "‑d".
03
Irregular Verb
The form changes completely.
04
Helping Verb
Supports the main verb (is, has, was…).
05
Transitive Verb
Eg: I am going to buy a car
06
Intransitive Verb
Actioned, but can't be touched.
Eg: She is crying
Base form → past → perfect: go (present) → went (past) → gone (perfect). Bonus from the same notes — three tenses: past, present, future.

Every morning, Hassan wakes up early and prepares himself for college. He usually walks to the bus stop, but today he is waiting for his friend because they are going to attend an important seminar. Adjective

8 types

The quality of a noun or subject. "He is a brave boy."

Types

01
Demonstrative Adjective
Points to a specific noun.
Eg: this, that, those, these — "This bag is mine." "Those chairs are old."
02
Descriptive Adjective
Describes the quality, kind, or condition of a noun.
Eg: a beautiful village, a yellow bus, fresh air
03
Possessive Adjective
Shows ownership or belonging.
Eg: my, our, your, his, her, its, their — "Our school." "Her camera." "My best friend."
04
Quantitative Adjective
Tells "how much", or a general (not exact) amount.
Eg: some, many, several, enough, much, little, each, every — "Several instructions." "Enough time." "Many paintings."
05
Numerical Adjective
Tells the exact number or order of nouns.
Eg: one, two, twenty, first, second — "Twenty students." "Three teachers." "Two hours."
06
Interrogative Adjective
Used before a noun to ask a question.
Eg: what, which, whose (only when directly followed by a noun) — "Which village did you visit?" "Whose bag is this?"
07
Comparative Adjective
Compares two nouns — usually ends in "‑er" or uses "more".
Eg: "This garden was bigger than the museum." "Sara's camera was newer than mine."
08
Superlative Adjective
Compares three or more nouns, showing the highest degree.
Eg: "Sara is my best friend."
PRACTICE PARAGRAPH
Last Sunday, our school arranged a wonderful educational trip to a beautiful historical village near the city. Twenty students and three teachers left the school early in the morning in a large yellow bus. Each student carried a small bag, a water bottle, and some healthy food for the journey. Our kind teacher, Mr. Ahmed, gave us several useful instructions before we started our exciting journey. He told us to stay together and take care of our personal belongings. After two hours, we reached a peaceful village surrounded by green fields and tall trees. The fresh air and cool weather made everyone happy. We visited an old museum where we saw many interesting paintings, ancient coins, and beautiful wooden chairs. A friendly guide showed us different rooms and told us amazing stories about the brave people who had lived there many years ago. Some students asked difficult questions, while other students carefully listened to the guide. After the museum visit, we walked towards a large garden with colorful flowers and soft grass. The teachers gave us enough time to enjoy the natural beauty of the place. My best friend, Sara, took many lovely pictures with her new camera. At lunchtime, we sat under a huge tree and shared our delicious food with one another. Every student enjoyed the tasty sandwiches, fresh fruit, and cold drinks. In the afternoon, we played several funny games and had a wonderful time together. Before returning home, each student bought a small traditional gift from the village market. It was a memorable day, and everyone felt proud of having such a useful and enjoyable experience.
Descriptive Quantitative / numerical Possessive
Written with no demonstrative (this, that, these, those) or interrogative (which, what, whose) adjectives — every adjective here is descriptive, quantitative, or possessive.

Adverb

4 types

Tells how, when, or where an action is done.

Types

01
Adverb of Manner
How the action is performed.
Eg: "She moves very quickly."
02
Adverb of Time
When the action happens.
Eg: "I will call you tomorrow."
03
Adverb of Place
Where the action happens.
Eg: "I will come here again."
04
Adverb of Frequency
How often the action is performed.

Preposition

3 types

Shows where — the relation between a noun and the rest of the sentence.

Types

01
Preposition of Time
Describing the time.
02
Preposition of Movement
The movement or change in distance.
03
Preposition of Place
A specific place.

Conjunction

3 types

Joins words, phrases, or sentences.

Types

01
Coordinate Conjunction
Eg: "Ali and Ahmed."
02
Subordinate Conjunction
Splits further into two kinds:
— Independent: gives a complete meaning on its own.
— Dependent: does not give a complete meaning on its own.
03
Co‑relative Conjunction
Works in a matched pair.
